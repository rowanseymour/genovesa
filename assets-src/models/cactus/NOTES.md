# The cactus

Why the shape is what it is; the numbers are in the file and the rules are in
the tests.

- **A candelabra, and that is the whole silhouette.** One column with arms that
  leave it sideways and turn straight back up. Nothing else in this world has a
  vertical edge — a palm is a stick with a head, a banana is a spray, a mangrove
  is an arch — so a fork of parallel uprights is recognisable at a distance
  where the other three are only proportions.
- **Narrow, and tall for its width.** Half the palm's height and a fifth of its
  spread. The ground it stands on is the emptiest in the world, so the plant has
  no neighbours to be seen against and its own proportion is all there is: a
  stouter column at the same height read as a stump.
- **Six sides, not more.** A facet has to be big enough to read as a facet, and
  a cactus is thinner than anything else here — at twelve the column is a smooth
  grey-green pole with nothing to catch the light. Real fluting is finer than
  that again and would be below the size of a pixel at any distance this is seen
  from, so it is not attempted.
- **No two arms alike.** They leave the column at their own heights, on their
  own bearings, and stop at their own tops. The server only turns a plant about
  the vertical, so three arms evenly spaced and equally tall would tile a whole
  collar — the palm's crown learned this first.
- **The column leans and is not quite straight**, for the same reason.
- **The arms start on the column's own axis**, so the two solids interpenetrate
  rather than meeting at a seam that would have to be mitred to stay closed.
- **A green nothing near it wears.** The arid collar is painted in scrub, parched
  and dust — an olive and two tans, the palest ground in the world — so the one
  colour that reads against all three is a dark blue-green, further from the
  grass than the forest is and much darker than the ground. It is the only green
  in that country, which is most of what makes a cactus findable across a bay.
- **The apex caps are a shade paler**, which is what a growing tip is, and it
  puts a highlight on each upright so a fork does not read as one solid mass.
- **No flower and no fruit.** The banana keeps its bunch because a red-purple
  pixel is unmistakable against green; here the ground behind the plant is
  already tan, so an accent that colour would be the second thing to see on a
  plant whose whole job is to be one vertical mark.
- **The foot is at the origin**, which is where the client puts it on the height
  field.
