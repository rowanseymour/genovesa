# The mangrove

Why the shape is what it is; the numbers are in the file and the rules are in
the tests.

- **The arch is the whole silhouette.** Nothing else in the world stands on
  legs, so a mangrove is recognisable at a distance that tells a palm from a
  banana only by its proportions. Prop roots leave the trunk going *outwards*
  and bend down into the mud, which is what a quadratic handle out at the
  foot's own radius does; lifting the middle of a straight chord instead gives
  splayed legs, and a cone of splayed legs is a camera tripod.
- **Uneven roots, uneven feet.** They meet the trunk at their own heights and
  land at their own reaches — an even ring of them reads as a birdcage.
- **One crown, not a cluster per limb.** Gathering lumps around each limb
  leaves gaps between the limbs, and the tree reads as three bushes sharing a
  stem. The lumps are scattered over one dome instead, on the golden angle so
  the crown never comes back round on itself: the server only turns a plant
  about the vertical, so a three-fold crown would tile a whole thicket.
- **Many small overlapping lumps, not a few big ones.** An octahedron is a
  crystal on its own and only stops being one where its neighbours cut its
  corners off. Eight triangles apiece buys that cheaply.
- **The crown overhangs its own footing**, which is what makes it a tree
  standing in water rather than a spider.
- **Canopy well below the banana's leaf and the palm's frond**, and blue where
  they are yellow — but set against the marsh and weed it stands on rather than
  against them. Those are the mid-toned tones, and what the eye picks the tree
  out by is that it is the dark thing on them.
- **Timber darker than the palm's**, because there are eight thin arches of it
  rather than one trunk: at the palm's value the same colour read as a bright
  tangle that took the crown's place as the thing you saw first.
- **The trunk carries on into the mud** rather than starting where the roots
  meet. The foot is at the origin, which is where the client puts it on the
  height field — and that height is the lake bed, so the roots are what hold
  the canopy over the water.

Where mangroves *stand* is `crates/world/src/mangroves.rs`, which will not put
one in deeper water than the arches are tall.
